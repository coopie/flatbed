# Statements taken so far
_Found: what Miss Dunmore, Jonas Pell and the lamplighter have said, and what Hale told the police._

- **Miss Clara Dunmore**, Hale's intended. Hale was at her mother's house in Union Street, Southwark, until a quarter to ten on Monday, then walked home alone. A week ago Captain Edward Lisle quarrelled with Garrow at the office, and Hale heard him say that a man like Garrow would be better dead.
- **Hale's landlady**, through the police. Hale came in to his lodging in Bermondsey at half past ten on Monday.
- **Peter Hale**, through the police. He left the office at six on Monday with Garrow alive and working. He found the body at seven on Tuesday. He does not know how his handkerchief came to be beside it; he thinks he left it on his desk. Holmes has not yet spoken to him.
- **Jonas Pell**, chandler. Shut his shop at eight on Monday, went to bed, heard nothing. Says the door at the top of his back stair "has not been opened since my father's time. Rusted fast."
- **Albert Coyle**, lamplighter. The gas company had been at the main and the lane's lamps were out. Relighting them at twenty past ten on Monday, he saw a light in Garrow's window. It was not the gas: it moved along the window and back, and bobbed, as a carried candle does, then went out.
- **Captain Edward Lisle**, through the police. Says he was at the Halberd Club in St James's the whole of Monday evening. Has a fresh cut across his knuckles, from a cab door, he says.
- **Mrs. Ruth Abney**, attic lodger. Lestrade calls her deaf and says she would tell him nothing. Nobody else has asked her.
