_My private working on the Garrow case: what I trust, what I doubt, and what nobody has asked._

**Tuesday night.**

*The ledger.* Forty pounds left in the bag; five leaves taken. A man who wanted money would have taken the bag. This was done for a page. Lestrade says it was Hale's page. Look again at which leaves are gone.

*The drops.* Mutton fat: tallow, a penny dip. Garrow burned gas and kept no candle. Someone brought a light into that room, and it was a cheap one.

*The light at 10.20.* Carried, not the gas, and the gas was off when Hale came. Whoever stood in that room at twenty past ten had put out the gas, and did not want the window lit.

*The bell.* It rings at every opening and shutting of the street door. Someone in that house would have heard it. Lestrade says the old woman is deaf. Lestrade says a great many things.

*The back door.* "Rusted fast," says Pell. I have seen only the office side of it.

*Lisle.* He lies about his evening; I would stake a good deal on it. But a man who lies is not always the man who killed. When did he come, and when did he go?

*Hale.* I have not seen him. A clerk knows his master's habits better than any ledger does. Garrow's effects went to the station, and I doubt Lestrade has looked at them.

*My suspicion* rests on two of these. It is not yet proof, and I shall not write the name even here until it is.

**To ask:** the attic. The far side of that door. Hale, and what was in the dead man's pockets. Lisle, once I have something to put to him.
