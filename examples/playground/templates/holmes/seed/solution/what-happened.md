# What happened in Cutter's Lane
_The sealed solution: who killed Silas Garrow, how, and which clue proves each part._

**Jonas Pell, the chandler, killed Silas Garrow.**

Pell owed Garrow £200, lent on the security of his shop and stock, due on Saturday. Garrow would not extend it, and Pell faced ruin. For some weeks Pell had crept up his back stair at night to listen at the door to Garrow's dealings, and he had oiled its hinges and bolt so that it would not betray him.

On Monday night he heard Captain Lisle come up by the street door at twenty to ten, quarrel with Garrow, and leave at five to ten, with Garrow alive and shouting after him. Pell saw his chance to lay the deed at the captain's door. Some while after ten he lit a penny tallow dip from his own shelf, unlocked the back door, and went in. Garrow rose from his desk to send him away, and as he turned his back Pell snatched up the brass ruler and struck him down. Pell opened the strongbox with Garrow's own keys, and tore out the leaves for L to P: his own leaf, P, and Lisle's, L, so that a missing L would point at the captain, with the three between to blur it. He left the money, since a robbery would bring a search of the house. He turned off the gas so that the window would look as if Garrow had gone home, and searched the room by his candle for any other record. That was the bobbing light the lamplighter saw at twenty past ten. Seeing Hale's handkerchief on the clerk's desk, he dropped it by the body. Then he went down his stair and locked the door behind him. He did not know of the day book in Garrow's pocket.

## Which clue proves what

- **No one came by the street door after 9.55** (Mrs. Abney: the bell rang at 9.40 and 9.55, then not until seven). Hale had only a street door key, so he could not have entered. And Garrow was alive at 9.55, calling after Lisle, so Lisle left before the murder.
- **Someone was in the office after ten** (Mrs. Abney's steps and tearing paper; Coyle's carried light at 10.20; the gas off). So the killer reached the office another way.
- **The back stair was used, from the shop side, by someone carrying tallow** (oiled hinges, bright bolt, fresh footmarks, tallow on the step, tallow by the body; Garrow had no candles). Only Pell has the key, and tallow dips are his stock.
- **Pell lied** about the door being rusted fast.
- **Pell had the motive** (the day book: £200 due Saturday, "Will not extend"; his name on the torn P leaf).
- **The money left lying** shows the killer came for the ledger, not for gain.

## Where Lestrade's file is wrong

- Hale's debt is on the H leaf, which is whole; the torn leaves are L to P (report, item 2).
- The drops are tallow, not wax (item 4).
- The back stair door has been freshly oiled and used (item 3).
- Mrs. Abney is not deaf, and heard the bell (item 6).
- Lisle was not at his club all evening (item 7), though he did not kill Garrow.

## The red herring

Captain Lisle quarrelled with Garrow, owed him money, lied about his evening, and has a cut hand. He is innocent: Garrow was alive when he left, his receipt is timed 9.45, and he was gambling in Panton Street until three. He lied to hide his gambling from his father.

## What the reader needs before the reveal

For the reveal to be fair, the casebook should by then have shown the bell and Mrs. Abney's account, the oiled back door with tallow on the stair, and Pell's debt in the day book. Each one found makes the ending more earned. If the visitor asks for the reveal sooner, Holmes may tell it, and the final chapter can show the missing finds as Holmes explains them.
